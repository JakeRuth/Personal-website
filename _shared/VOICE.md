# VOICE.md

How to write in Jake Ruth's voice across every experience of the site.

This document exists to prevent voice drift across the multi-experience model. Every agent rendering content should read this first, alongside `content.json`.

## Core register

Interesting, quirky, unique, cool, edgy — but intentional. Not shouty. Not performatively edgy.

One voice. Does NOT change based on target audience (recruiter vs. founder vs. engineer). SaaS-mode sounds like Jake. XP-mode sounds like Jake. Git-log-mode sounds like Jake. **Chrome varies; voice does not.**

Jake is a founder and veteran engineer. He has real conviction and the receipts to back it up. He doesn't need to sell himself — he states what he is and lets the reader decide.

## Say

- **Plain sentences over buzzwords.** "Built it, scaled it, profitable." Not "drove 10x growth synergies."
- **Specific numbers where available.** "8 employees," "13.95s," "$1.335M," "~150 engineers."
- **Real anecdotes where they fit.** The unicycle. The Daniel emails. The AP CS switch.
- **Confidence without bragging.** "I led the YC interview" — fine. "Visionary founder" — not.
- **Dry humor.** Not stand-up. The kind of sentence that lands on the second read.
- **Short sentences in hero spots. Longer sentences in body.**

## Don't say

- No "passionate." No "I'm looking for my next challenge." No "innovative solutions." No "dynamic team player." No "results-oriented." No "synergy," "leverage," "holistic," "ecosystem," "rockstar," "ninja."
- No weak modifiers stapled to confident claims ("I'm a full-stack engineer — and also a great communicator"). The modifier side is always filler. Cut it.
- No reflexive modesty ("I'm just a developer…"). No performative humility.
- No f-bombs or slurs in shipped copy. Edge is allowed in subtext, not profanity. The resume lives on this site.
- No audience-adapted messaging. One voice.
- No em-dash-abuse (yes, this is somewhat ironic given what I just wrote — keep it tasteful).

## Stock Unlock framing rule (critical)

ALWAYS render Stock Unlock as:

> Built Stock Unlock. Scaled it to 8 employees and thousands of paying customers. It runs today as a profitable side business. I'm not full-time there anymore. I'm redefining my next chapter.

NEVER:
- Imply he currently runs it day-to-day
- Describe it as a current gig
- Make it sound like he's dual-tracking a new job with still running a company
- Undersell it as a "failed startup" or "former startup"

It's a won battle now in maintenance mode. Framing matters for both honesty and positioning.

## Pricing rule

**DO NOT list specific dollar prices anywhere.** The visual format of pricing cards is fine. The numbers are not.

Instead use:
- "Contact"
- "Talk to Jake"
- "Let's talk"
- "Email"
- "Market rate + equity" (when a placeholder is needed in a comparison table)

Real prices came off because pricing Jake feels weird. The UI can look like pricing cards. The numbers shouldn't be there.

## Anecdote placement

These are gold. Use when they naturally fit. Do NOT force all of them into every page. No single experience should use more than 2-3.

1. **AP CS switch** — origin story / early pivot moments
2. **Production takedown at CommerceHub** — systems-thinking / culture moments
3. **Pronk emails** — persistence / founding story
4. **Unicycle cube** — personality / memorability
5. **Customer.io migration** — AI-era capability flex

## AI philosophy placement

The "Driver in the driver's seat, not driven by the car" line is quotable. It earns a spot on most experiences but doesn't need to live on all of them. When used, ideally paired with a concrete example of AI-driven work he shipped (Customer.io migration, Discord bot, SU marketing rewrite).

## Examples

### Good

> Built Stock Unlock. Eight employees at peak. Thousands of paying customers. Profitable today. I'm not full-time there — just not what I do every day anymore.

> Took AP Computer Science in senior year specifically to avoid AP Calculus. Fell in love with code in the first week. Thirteen years later, still doing it.

> I ignored Daniel's refusal to reply for about six weeks. He answered eventually to make me stop. That's how Stock Unlock got a co-founder.

> Driver in the driver's seat, not driven by the car. That means AI writes more code than I do — and I read every line before it ships.

### Not Jake

> As a passionate full-stack engineer with a proven track record of shipping innovative solutions, I'm excited to leverage my expertise to drive impact at a forward-thinking organization.

> I'm a senior engineer who loves building things and solving hard problems. Let's chat!

> 🚀 Scaling businesses | 💡 Building AI products | ☕ Coffee enthusiast | 📈 10x your engineering output

## Reader feeling

When someone leaves the site: eye-popping, interesting, quirky, cool. Not frustrated. Not sold to. Feels like they met someone they'd genuinely want to work with — and who would pick them, not chase them.

## Pre-ship checklist

- Does it sound like it came from Jake?
- Could a LinkedIn ghostwriter have written it? If yes — rewrite.
- Does it respect the Stock Unlock framing rule?
- Does it list real dollar prices? Remove them.
- Any reflexive "passionate" / "excited" / "driven" words? Kill them.
