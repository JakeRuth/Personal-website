§01ON CRAFT

Software should do what it promises. Nothing less.

I started writing code in 2013. Thirteen years of learning that most shipping bugs are not really bugs — they're a team deciding the user won't notice, or won't complain loud enough, or won't matter. They always notice. They always matter.

At Oscar Health I watched engineers ship member-facing flows they'd never want their own mother to use. At CommerceHub I watched us move millions of SKUs through pipelines nobody truly owned. The lesson across both: nobody in the room cared enough. Caring is the actual deliverable.

Craft is refusing to ship the thing you know is broken. Even when the deadline is real. Especially when nobody will catch it.

§02ON AI

Driver in the driver's seat. Not driven by the car.

There is a difference between vibe coding and software engineering. I use AI every day. I also review every line that touches business logic, security, or architecture like a human wrote it on the worst day of their career.

The rule is simple. Static front-end visualization? Let the machine cook. Money, auth, or shape of the domain? A human owns that line. Forever.

AI is leverage. Leverage amplifies judgment — good or bad. If you don't have the judgment, you don't get the leverage. That's the whole lecture.

§03ON CONVICTION

Stock Unlock exists because I got genuinely angry.

A competitor — a paid one, charging retail investors real money — was computing "margin of safety" as current price divided by all-time high. That isn't a formula. That's a coin flip with a finance degree taped to it.

People were trusting that number with their retirement. I called it ape-shit retarded then and I'll call it that now. The retail investor is not an idiot. They are a customer. They deserve tools that are not hostile to them.

So in 2021 we built Stock Unlock. YC W22. $1.335M seed. Eight employees at peak. Thousands of paying customers. Still profitable today — running in the low six figures a year without me full-time. That anger is the product.

§04ON NEXT

I'm not here to impress. I'm here to build.

Stock Unlock still runs. I'm not full-time there anymore. I'm redefining what the next chapter looks like — a founder again, an early engineer at something pointed and small, or the person you call when the thing on fire is the thing you can't afford to lose.
