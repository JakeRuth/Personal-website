Chapter One

The case for one engineer.

This is a magazine about one engineer. The argument is short: most teams need fewer people who ship more, not more people who ship less. Here are the receipts.

Thirteen years. Four companies. One YC batch. Jake shipped his first production code in 2013 at CommerceHub, took down production once as an intern, and was told by his manager to stop apologizing — "that wasn’t your fault, you shouldn’t have had access to that button." That sentence is the whole job, said out loud.

He joined Oscar Health at around fifty engineers and left at roughly a hundred and fifty. Leveled Associate to Mid to Senior, declined the manager track twice, and kept shipping Python and Go behind the product. In 2021 he left to co-found Stock Unlock, led the YC interview, closed a $1.335M seed, scaled the team to eight and the customer list to thousands. Today it runs as a profitable side business. He is not full-time there. He is redefining what comes next.

If that’s the kind of person you’d want on day one, keep reading. If it isn’t, the rest of the internet is forgiving.

Chapter Three

POST /api/hire

The API has one endpoint. It is documented like a magazine ought to document things — plainly, with a worked example, no pricing table in the payload.

Send a short brief. Get a reply within a business day. Pricing is not in the request body; it happens in the conversation after the reply. That conversation is usually short.

  • Response posture: fast during ET business hours.
  • Preferred channel: email. DMs are a last resort.
  • Pricing lives in a follow-up, not a landing page.

Dispatch

Stock Unlock, in its own words.

Built it. Scaled it to eight employees and thousands of paying customers. It runs today as a profitable side business, with three full-time employees keeping the lights on. I’m not full-time there anymore. I’m redefining my next chapter.

The origin beat, for the record: Daniel Pronk, an investing YouTuber, spent five to ten hours every weekend on Excel sheets. I wrote a Python automation in a weekend and emailed it to him. He ignored me for six weeks. He answered eventually to make me stop. That’s how Stock Unlock got a co-founder.
